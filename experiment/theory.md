<h3>Extrusion process</h3>

<p>
Hot extrusion is a metal forming process of forcing a heated billet to be reduced in its cross section by forcing it to flow through a shaped die opening under a high pressure. During extrusion metal billet is under compression stress state in all three direction and shear forces, figure 1. No tensile force is produced, which makes high deformation possible without tearing the metal. The hot extrusion is the widely used due to its relative low deformation resistance of the metal for production of long straight metal products of constant cross section (such as bars, solid and hollow sections, tubes, wires and strips) from materials that can not be formed by cold extrusion.

<h2>Uses:</h2>

<ol>
    <li>Domestic water systems.</li>
    <li>Pipelines transporting gas or liquid over long distances.</li>
    <li>Scaffolding</li>
    <li>Structural steel</li>

    <li>As components in mechanical systems such as:
        <ul>
            <li>Rollers in conveyor belts</li>
            <li>Compactors (e.g., steam rollers)</li>
            <li>Bearing casing</li>
        </ul>
    </li>

    <li>The petroleum industry:
        <ul>
            <li>Oil well casing</li>
            <li>Oil refinery equipment</li>
        </ul>
    </li>
</ol>
